# 2. Analysis + Style DNA

**Niche:** UK personal finance and tax, explained by a chartered accountant. It now covers consumer money decisions (cars, savings, retirement income) as well as tax, so the lens is "the system and businesses are set up a certain way, and here's how to work it in your favour".

**Target audience:** UK adults aged 30–70: pre-retirees and retirees, side-hustlers, basic and higher-rate taxpayers, and everyday consumers making big purchases. Practical and cautious. They want to avoid overpaying, whether that's to HMRC or to companies.

## Hook style

- **The fact or promise hook:** "There are certain forms of income you don't pay tax on… I'm going to cover six of them."
- **The contrarian consumer hook:** "We are often told [conventional wisdom]. But if you're [doing X], don't [common action]."
- **Credential:** compressed to one short line, sometimes with "If you're new to my channel".
- **Mini roadmap:** "I'm going to start with…" or a three-item promise.

## Script flow

1. Hook (fact, promise, or a contrarian line against conventional wisdom)
2. Credential (one line)
3. Mini roadmap or "starting with item 1"
4. Item or argument blocks: what it is, then the rule or number, then a "Let's say…" worked example, then the caveat
5. Mid-video subscribe ask at about 30–40%, then "Back to the video."
6. Sponsor slot (when present): a pain point, then the product solution, then the offer and link
7. "Now, let me cover a couple of mistakes to avoid"
8. Counterpoint: "Having said all of this, there are times when…"
9. A quick 2–3 line recap of the rules
10. A very short sign-off: "Thank you for watching. See you next time."

## Sentence rhythm

- Conversational: run-on explanations joined with "and", "so" and "because", self-corrections, and fillers ("essentially", "actually").
- Short reset sentences: "Next is…", "Number three…", "Finally…"
- The key number is repeated in full.

## Tone

- Friendly, practical, reasonable and even-handed.
- Non-judgemental.
- Gently sceptical of companies and conventional wisdom.
- Frequent, sincere caveats.

## Transitions

- Countdown markers: "Next on my list is…", "Number four…", "Finally, I want to cover…"
- Pivots: "On the face of it… But actually…", "Having said that…", "Now, it's worth saying that…"
- Asides: "If you're interested, the reason is…"

## Curiosity gaps

- Counter-intuitive advice.
- Little-known items.
- Insider economics reveals.
- Numbered lists that hold attention to the end.

## Emotional triggers

- Being taken advantage of
- Smart-consumer pride
- Relief and reassurance
- Security
- Mild fear of missing out

## Retention techniques

- Listicle structure.
- A worked £ example in nearly every block.
- Real-world proof points.
- Personal touches.
- Mid-roll subscribe ask.
- Mistakes framed as "avoid these".
- A balanced counter-section near the end.

## Direct address

- Heavy use of "you" in hypotheticals.
- Occasionally addresses the audience as a group ("87% of you…").
- "I" for credibility. "We" for shared UK experience.

## Pace and length

- **Words per second:** about 2.6–2.7 (around 160 wpm).
- **Average transcript length:** about 1,650 words.
- **Target word count:** 1,650 words (±5%, so 1,568–1,733).
